In short. When these models get a knapsack or colouring instance wrong, their answer is often exactly what a simple greedy procedure gives: ratio or value greedy on knapsack, and colouring the vertices in the order they are listed. Thinking at high effort turns Qwen3's answers from mostly invalid into mostly valid, and mostly correct on most units. Its wrong colourings are often exactly listing-order first fit's: on about half the items where both are wrong. But a mixture of heuristics run exactly, plus near misses, does not describe the answers. The only fits that reproduce their data are ones where the model almost always solves the problem. The models also often call solvable instances impossible. And gpt-oss-120b at low effort answers the textbook problem when a connective is redefined, until the rule is restated. All of this is preliminary: dev items, one sample per item, nothing pre-registered.

  1. When the models get knapsack wrong, their answer is often a greedy rule's exact answer, far more often than chance. Take the items where both the model and RATIO-SKIP are wrong. RATIO-SKIP adds items by value per unit weight and skips any that do not fit. On those items, gpt-oss-20b at low effort gives RATIO-SKIP's packing on 29 of 112, and gpt-oss-120b on 35 of 79. The permutation chance levels are 0.008 and 0.009, and p = 0.0005, the smallest possible with 2,000 permutations. Difference greedy (DIFF) and value greedy (VALUE) show the same for gpt-oss-20b: 27 of 125 and 19 of 101. Qwen3 with thinking off does it too, but less often: Qwen3-14B gives RATIO-SKIP's packing on 23 of 223 (chance 0.007), and Qwen3-32B gives the ratio-prefix packing on 15 of 266 (chance 0.005). With thinking, Qwen3 is wrong on only 10 of 296 items (Qwen3-32B) and 8 of 278 (Qwen3-14B), too few to say.

    No interval: these are counts, one sample per item. Chance level: the within-cell permutation baseline. (c) descriptive count, tested against permutation chance

  2. On C E2 colouring, a wrong colouring that equals any member's is almost always first fit in the listed vertex order. FF-LIST takes the vertices in the order the prompt lists them and gives each the first colour that fits; on these items it is identical to greedy with repair in the same order. Of the wrong colourings that equal some member's, FF-LIST's account for 64 of 64 for gpt-oss-120b at low effort, 40 of 41 for gpt-oss-20b at low effort, 99 of 111 for Qwen3-14B with thinking and 80 of 100 for Qwen3-32B with thinking. Most wrong colourings equal no member's. Where both are wrong, the model's colouring equals FF-LIST's:

    • Qwen3-14B with thinking: 99 of 199 items;
    • Qwen3-32B with thinking: 80 of 177;
    • gpt-oss-120b at low effort: 64 of 225;
    • gpt-oss-20b at low effort: 40 of 232.

    The same greedy rule in other vertex orders rarely matches. In the order above:

    • label order (FF-LABEL): 2 of 181, 0, 0 and 0;
    • largest degree first (LF): 11 of 185, 20 of 172, 0 and 2;
    • DSATUR: 7 of 115, 6 of 105, 0 and 0.

    With thinking off, no Qwen3 colouring equals any member's: Qwen3-32B gives 1 proper colouring in 300, and Qwen3-14B 66.

    No interval: counts, one sample per item. No permutation baseline: no two items in a cell share their vertex labels, so the within-cell shuffle cannot move any answer. A uniformly random colouring would equal a given member's with probability below 10−12. That is not the right chance level for a model that colours sensibly, so the other vertex orders serve as the control. (c) descriptive count

  3. Thinking turns Qwen3's answers from mostly invalid to mostly valid, and mostly correct on most units. Correct answers out of ok answers, for Qwen3-32B on the same pc20 items, thinking off → on:

    • C E2 knapsack: 1/300 → 286/296;
    • C E2 colouring: 0/300 → 106/290 (proper colourings: 1 → 280);
    • C E1 colouring: 1/120 → 113/119;
    • C E1 3-SAT: 6/116 → 66/117;
    • A/H E5 entailment: 8/80 → 75/78.

    Qwen3-14B shows the same: C E2 knapsack 11/300 → 270/278, C E2 colouring 0/300 → 98/299 (proper colourings: 66 → 291), and C E1 colouring 0/120 → 110/120. With thinking, each model is correct on most items in 6 of the 9 pc20 units. The exceptions are C E2 colouring (above), C E2 3-SAT (37/192 and 59/187) and A/H E5 SAT (20/95 and 22/95). On C E5 SAT with a partial assignment given (pc40), Qwen3-32B goes from 5/120 to 93/117, and Qwen3-14B from 1/120 to 104/118.

    The fitted C E5 mixtures show the same shift. With thinking off, they put their largest weight on POLARITY, which ignores the given assignment: 0.567 [0.435, 0.703] for Qwen3-32B and 0.483 [0.329, 0.644] for Qwen3-14B, with ε at its cap. With thinking, the largest weight is on EXACT: 0.892 [0.810, 0.958] and 0.928 [0.850, 0.989].

    No interval on the counts: one sample per item. Intervals on the weights: 95% percentile. Chance level: none (within-model comparison). The direct condition's system message conflicts with the prompts (see Limits), which may depress direct accuracy. (c) descriptive count; the two thinking fits are (a), the direct fits (b)

  4. A mixture of heuristics run exactly, plus near misses, rarely describes the answers. Of 24 fits, 14 have the slip rate ε at its cap (0.5). Only 4 pass every check against their data: Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-14B with thinking, on C E2 knapsack and on C E5 SAT with a partial assignment. All four have EXACT at 0.89 or more, so their heuristic weights are small. Where errors are common, the near-miss model gets their shape wrong:

    • gpt-oss-20b's wrong knapsack packings are often two items from the nearest member, one item swapped for another: 0.15 of answers, against 0.08 predicted (distance-profile p = 0.01).
    • gpt-oss-120b's knapsack fit is borderline: p = 0.0495, just under the 0.05 line, with the same excess of two-item answers (0.07 against 0.04).
    • The two Qwen3 colouring fits with thinking have ε well below the cap (0.15 and 0.13). Their largest weight is on FF-LIST: 0.373 [0.313, 0.436] for Qwen3-32B and 0.464 [0.398, 0.531] for Qwen3-14B. But each expects 0.03–0.04 of answers beyond every member's reach, and there are none. Each also expects fewer exact matches than the data have: 0.66 against 0.71, and 0.65 against 0.70. With thinking, the answers are more procedure-like than the near-miss model allows.

    Check: each fit is compared with 100 fake data sets drawn from it. The checks are conservative, so a failure is a real misfit. The distance-profile check was added on 2026-11-18, after we saw that the earlier checks could barely fail; it makes verdicts stricter, not looser. (b) fitted, but the model does not reproduce the data, except the four named (a)

  5. The models often call a solvable instance impossible. Every C E5 3-colouring graph at pc40 is 3-colourable, yet the answer is IMPOSSIBLE on most of them:

    • gpt-oss-20b at low effort: 193 of 200;
    • gpt-oss-120b at low effort: 160 of 200;
    • Qwen3-32B with thinking: 134 of 197;
    • Qwen3-14B with thinking: 119 of 186;
    • Qwen3-32B with thinking off: 99 of 199.

    Qwen3-14B with thinking off says IMPOSSIBLE only once in 199, but none of its colourings is proper.

    gpt-oss-20b at low effort also answers UNSAT on satisfiable formulas: 53 of 110 on C E1 3-SAT (pc20) and 68 of 119 on C E5 3-SAT (pc40), where every formula is satisfiable.

    These answers fit "colour greedily with three colours and give up at the first dead end". On these items, though, that rule and "always say impossible" give nearly the same answers, so the data cannot tell them apart. That is also why the unit is not fitted.

    No interval: counts, one sample per item. Chance level: none. (c) descriptive count

  6. When a connective is redefined, gpt-oss-120b at low effort answers the textbook problem, and restating the rule fixes most of it. On A/H E5 entailment, the prompt redefines "either A or B" to mean that neither holds. Under that inverted rule, gpt-oss-120b at low effort gives the textbook (inclusive-or) answer on 16 of 20 items and the correct one on none. With the rule restated just before the question, the counts are 3 textbook and 16 correct out of 20. gpt-oss-20b gives 3 textbook answers in 18 (8 correct), and 1 in 17 once the rule is restated (14 correct). With thinking, Qwen3-32B is correct on 17 of 20 under the inversion, with 3 textbook answers; Qwen3-14B on 14 of 19, with 4. Once the rule is restated, they are correct on 19 of 19 and 19 of 20, with no textbook answer. On the inverted knapsack and colouring rules, textbook answers are absent or nearly so (at most 1 in 20 per run).

    In the eval's terms, restating the rule removes most textbook answers. That points to the redefinition being lost by the time the model answers (retention), rather than a fixed prior for the textbook reading. The counts are small, though.

    No interval: counts, about 20 items per framing, one sample each. Chance level: none; under the inverted framings, the textbook answer and the correct one are disjoint by construction. (c) descriptive count

    Its correct shares on those units describe only the items it finished, which are likely the easier ones; the tables mark them †.
