What we can say so far

Hand-written claims (docs/results/findings.html). Strength labels: (a) fitted, and the fitted model reproduces the data; (b) fitted, but the model does not reproduce the data, so the weights read as nearness; (c) a descriptive count.

In short. Some wrong answers are exactly what a simple greedy procedure gives. The clearest case is colouring: when a wrong colouring equals any member's, it is usually first fit in the listed vertex order, 40 to 99 times per run against about 1 expected from a random-order first fit. On knapsack, gpt-oss's wrong packings often equal a greedy rule's, but mostly because they are near-optimal; the part specific to greedy is modest. Qwen3 in the thinking condition is correct far more often than in the direct condition, and its wrong colourings are often first fit's. A mixture of heuristics run exactly, plus near misses, rarely reproduces the data: 4 of 24 fits pass every check, all near ceiling (79–97% correct), and that count depends on a check added after seeing the data. The models often call 3-colourable graphs impossible, but not where list-order greedy gets stuck. At high effort, gpt-oss rarely answers wrongly but often runs out of tokens: of the 542 dev items gpt-oss-20b got wrong at low effort, 190 become correct, 347 hit the token cap and 4 stay wrong. Under a redefined connective, gpt-oss-120b at low effort mostly answers the textbook problem until the rule is restated. All of this is preliminary: dev items, one sample per item, nothing pre-registered.

  1. On C E2 colouring, a wrong colouring that equals any member's is usually first fit in the listed vertex order. FF-LIST takes the vertices in the order the prompt lists them and gives each the first colour that fits; on these items it is identical to greedy with repair in the same order. Of the wrong colourings that equal some member's, FF-LIST's account for:

    • 64 of 64 for gpt-oss-120b at low effort;
    • 40 of 41 for gpt-oss-20b at low effort;
    • 99 of 111 for Qwen3-14B in the thinking condition;
    • 80 of 100 for Qwen3-32B in the thinking condition.

    For gpt-oss, most wrong colourings equal no member's (173 of 237 and 215 of 256). For Qwen3 in the thinking condition, just over half equal one (111 of 201 and 100 of 184).

    Where both are wrong, the model's colouring equals FF-LIST's on 99 of 199, 80 of 177, 64 of 225 and 40 of 232 items. A random-order first fit (the stored RO-FF distribution) would give FF-LIST's wrong colouring on about 1 item per run (0.7–1.2): that is the chance level for a model that colours greedily in some order. The same rule in other orders matches far less often:

    • largest-first (LF): 11 and 20 times for Qwen3 in the thinking condition, against about 0.7 by chance;
    • DSATUR: 7 and 6 times, against about 0.6;
    • label order (FF-LABEL): 2 and 0 times;
    • for gpt-oss, 0–2 times each.

    The listed order is also the order the answer must be written in, so this shows the answers follow the listed order. It does not show whether that comes from reading or from writing. In the direct condition, no Qwen3 colouring equals any member's: Qwen3-32B gives 1 proper colouring in 300, Qwen3-14B 66.

    Counts, without intervals; one sample per item. Chance level: the RO-FF distribution, matched to each item's graph (analysis/findings_checks.py); this unit has no within-cell permutation baseline, because no two items in a cell share vertex labels. (c) descriptive count

  2. gpt-oss's wrong knapsack packings often equal a greedy rule's, but mostly because they are near-optimal. At low effort, 50 of gpt-oss-120b's 88 wrong packings and 57 of gpt-oss-20b's 142 equal some greedy member's packing. On these items the greedy rules are wrong by design, so their packings are often the runner-up by value, and many of the models' wrong packings are the runner-up (49 of 88 and 61 of 142). A null that keeps each run's own spread over value ranks, but ignores which packings are greedy, predicts 33 and 46 greedy matches. The excess, 17 and 11, is the part specific to greedy.

    It also shows in a contrast. When the runner-up packing is a greedy one, gpt-oss-120b gives it on 32 of 45 wrong items; when it is not, on 17 of 43. For gpt-oss-20b the counts are 40 of 78 and 21 of 64. The greedy rules often give the same packing, so the data cannot say which rule.

    The within-cell permutation chance level makes the agreement look much stronger (RATIO-SKIP: 35 of 79 against 0.009, for example) because it ignores near-optimality. The near-misses are swaps: 44 of gpt-oss-20b's 45 wrong packings that sit two items from the nearest member swap one item for another.

    Qwen3 in the direct condition gives a greedy member's packing on 36 of 289 and 23 of 299 wrong answers, against 17 and 8 under the null. Many of its packings are infeasible (126 and 28). In the thinking condition it is wrong too rarely to say (8 of 278 and 10 of 296).

    Counts, without intervals. Null: each run's own distribution of value ranks (analysis/findings_checks.py). (c) descriptive count

  3. In the thinking condition, Qwen3 is correct far more often than in the direct condition. Qwen3-32B on the same pc20 items, direct → thinking, correct out of ok answers:

    • C E2 knapsack: 1/300 → 286/296;
    • C E2 colouring: 0/300 → 106/290;
    • C E1 colouring: 1/120 → 113/119;
    • C E1 3-SAT: 6/116 → 66/117;
    • A/H E5 entailment: 8/80 → 75/78.

    Qwen3-14B shows the same: C E2 knapsack 11/300 → 270/278 (270 of all 300 responses; 22 were parse failures), C E2 colouring 0/300 → 98/299, C E1 colouring 0/120 → 110/120. In the thinking condition each model is correct on most items in 6 of the 9 pc20 units. The exceptions are C E2 colouring, C E2 3-SAT (37/192 and 59/187) and A/H E5 SAT (20/95 and 22/95). On C E5 SAT with a partial assignment given (pc40), Qwen3-32B goes from 5/120 to 93/117, and Qwen3-14B from 1/120 to 104/118.

    Validity is mixed. Colourings and C E1 assignments go from mostly invalid to valid. Direct knapsack packings were already mostly feasible (272/300 and 174/300). SAT assignments stay mostly invalid in the thinking condition (A/H E5 20/86 and 22/78; C E2 28/167 and 38/130).

    The fitted C E5 mixtures shift the same way. In the direct condition their largest weight is on POLARITY, which ignores the given assignment: 0.567 [0.435, 0.703] for Qwen3-32B and 0.483 [0.329, 0.644] for Qwen3-14B, with ε at its cap. In the thinking condition it is on EXACT: 0.892 [0.810, 0.958] and 0.928 [0.850, 0.989].

    The two conditions differ in four ways at once (RUNNER.md, Conditions):

    • reasoning on or off;
    • a token cap of 32,768 or 1,024;
    • no system message, or one asking for no working;
    • Qwen3's thinking or non-thinking sampling settings.

    So this compares conditions, not thinking alone. Nothing here shows that the high effort setting matters for Qwen3. Scorer 1.5.1 was written after seeing 98 parse failures on Qwen3-14B's pc20 knapsack answers; it recovered them, and 96 of the 98 are correct.

    Counts, without intervals. Intervals on the weights: 95% percentile. Chance level: none (a within-model comparison). (c) descriptive count; the two thinking-condition C E5 fits are (a), the direct fits (b)

  4. A mixture of heuristics run exactly, plus near misses, rarely reproduces the data. Of 24 fits, 14 have the slip rate ε at its cap (0.5). Only 4 pass every check against their data: Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-14B in the thinking condition, on C E2 knapsack and on C E5 SAT with a partial assignment. All four are near ceiling (79–97% correct, with 8–24 wrong answers each), so their heuristic weights are small, and the checks have little power there. Recovery at their own estimates also fails the coverage gate on a small weight in all four (minimum coverage 0.37–0.89).

    This count depends on the distance-profile check, which compares how far the answers sit from the nearest member with how far fake data from the fit sit. It was added on 2026-11-18, after we saw that the earlier checks could barely fail while ε is below its cap. It changes exactly two verdicts, the gpt-oss knapsack fits: p = 0.0099 for gpt-oss-20b and 0.0495 for gpt-oss-120b, the second a failure by one fake data set. Without it, 6 of 24 fits pass. It reverses the earlier write-up, which called gpt-oss-20b's knapsack fit the one that reproduces its data.

    Where errors are common, the misfit has a shape:

    • gpt-oss-20b's wrong packings sit two items from the nearest member (a one-for-one swap) on 0.15 of answers, against 0.08 predicted; the slip model makes one-item slips more likely than two-item ones.
    • The two Qwen3 colouring fits in the thinking condition have ε well below the cap (0.15 and 0.13), and their largest weight is on FF-LIST: 0.373 [0.313, 0.436] and 0.464 [0.398, 0.531]. But they predict 0.03–0.04 of answers beyond every member's reach, and there are 0 and 1 of 299. They also predict fewer exact matches than the data have: 0.66 against 0.71, and 0.65 against 0.70.

    Check: each fit is compared with 100 fake data sets drawn from it, on four statistics. A single marginal failure is weak evidence; the clear failures are those with ε at its cap or with several checks failed. (b) fitted, but the model does not reproduce the data, except the four named (a)

  5. The models often call a 3-colourable graph impossible, but not where list-order greedy gets stuck. All 200 C E5 3-colouring graphs (pc40) are 3-colourable. 160 were selected so that list-order first fit with three colours gets stuck. The other 40 are controls, where it succeeds. IMPOSSIBLE answers, stuck graphs against controls:

    • gpt-oss-20b at low effort: 156 of 160 against 37 of 40;
    • gpt-oss-120b at low effort: 131 of 160 against 29 of 40;
    • Qwen3-32B in the thinking condition: 109 of 157 against 25 of 40;
    • Qwen3-14B in the thinking condition: 101 of 149 against 18 of 37;
    • Qwen3-32B in the direct condition: 79 of 159 against 20 of 40, about half.

    Qwen3-14B in the direct condition almost never says IMPOSSIBLE (1 of 199), but none of its colourings is proper.

    So the give-ups do not track list-order dead ends. They are equally consistent with an edge-order greedy variant that gives up on 194 of the 200 graphs and with "always impossible"; the data cannot separate those two, which is why the unit is not fitted.

    gpt-oss-20b at low effort likewise answers UNSAT on satisfiable formulas: 53 of 110 on C E1 3-SAT (pc20), and 68 of 119 on C E5 3-SAT (pc40). The C E5 cases are again flat across cells: 23 of 40 on controls, 26 of 40 on dead-end formulas and 19 of 39 on their permutations.

    At high effort, gpt-oss-20b gives up far less where it finishes, but often does not finish (dev, 5 items per cell, one sample each; claim 7). Of the 275 wrong IMPOSSIBLE or UNSAT answers it gave at low effort, at high effort 64 become correct, 1 stays wrong and 210 run out of tokens, including 145 of the 150 on C E3. For example:

    • On the 25 C E5 3-colouring items, it colours none at low effort. At high effort it colours all 18 it finishes, 17 of which it had called impossible, and runs out of tokens on the other 7.
    • On C E1 3-SAT, it solves 22 of the 23 items both runs answered. Low effort solved none of them: it answered UNSAT on 8 and gave invalid assignments on 15.

    Counts, without intervals. Chance level: none; the control cell is the comparison. (c) descriptive count

  6. Under a redefined connective, gpt-oss-120b at low effort answers the textbook problem; a restatement just before the question removes most of it. On A/H E5 entailment, the prompt redefines "either A or B" to mean that neither holds. Under that inverted rule (I), gpt-oss-120b at low effort gives the textbook (inclusive-or) answer on 16 of 20 items and the correct one on none. With a reworded restatement of the rule just before the question (I+R), it gives 3 textbook answers and 16 correct. Paired by base instance, 13 switch away from the textbook answer and none switch to it (exact McNemar p = 0.0002).

    The other runs:

    • gpt-oss-20b at low effort: 3 textbook answers of 18 under I;
    • Qwen3 in the thinking condition: 3 and 4 of 20 and 19 under I, none after the restatement;
    • Qwen3 in the direct condition: 3 and 4 under both framings, though it is rarely correct anywhere on this unit.

    Under the inverted knapsack and colouring rules, textbook answers are absent or nearly so (at most 1 in 20 per run). Under the inverted SAT rule, Qwen3 in the thinking condition gives a textbook solution on 3 of 18 and 3 of 20 items, but it rarely solves the stated problem either (1 and 2 correct).

    I+R differs from I by a second, reworded statement of the rule. So the drop is consistent with the model losing the redefinition before it answers (retention), or with the clearer wording. The eval's trace coding, which would separate the two, has not been done. The by-framing breakdown was added after the runs, and this run and domain (1 of 24 at pc20) is reported because it stood out.

    Counts, about 20 items per framing, one sample each; the paired test is in analysis/findings_checks.py. (c) descriptive count

  7. At high effort, gpt-oss rarely answers wrongly, but it often runs out of tokens. On the dev items, with a 32,768-token cap and one sample per item at temperature 1:

    • gpt-oss-120b: 284 of 1,270 responses truncated; 969 of the other 986 correct;
    • gpt-oss-20b: 462 of 1,270 truncated; of the other 808, 799 correct, 7 wrong and 2 unparseable.

    The truncations concentrate on a few units (gpt-oss-120b, then gpt-oss-20b): C E3's uninstructed colouring gadgets (COL-U) 143 and 174 of 180, A/H E2 SAT 38 and 39 of 40, C E2 3-SAT 29 and 42 of 50, C E2 knapsack 14 and 69 of 75.

    gpt-oss-20b also ran the dev items at low effort. The two runs send identical requests except for the effort setting, so its answers pair by item:

    • Counting a truncated or unparseable response as not correct, high effort is right on 799 of the 1,270 items and low effort on 716. 198 are right only at high effort and 115 only at low, 111 of those because high effort ran out of tokens (exact McNemar p = 3×10−6). The net gain hides losses: on C E2 knapsack it is 6 against 34 of 75.
    • On the 798 items both runs answered, low effort gets 604 right and high effort 791 (190 against 3). But which items high effort finishes goes with how easy they are: low effort was wrong on 24% of these 798, and on 76% of the 458 that high effort left unfinished.
    • Of the 542 items it got wrong at low effort, at high effort it gets 190 right, runs out of tokens on 347 and gets 4 wrong, each with a different wrong answer; 1 is unparseable.
    • Low effort's wrong answers that equal some member's (196) are of three kinds. 114 are IMPOSSIBLE or UNSAT give-ups: at high effort 55 become correct, 58 run out of tokens and 1 stays wrong. 59 are full colourings, assignments or packings: 11 correct, 47 out of tokens, 1 wrong. 23 are decisions and other short answers: 17 correct, 6 out of tokens.

    So with more reasoning, gpt-oss-20b does not trade one wrong answer for another: where it finishes, the answer is almost always right. But low effort's greedy-looking constructions mostly turn into truncations at high effort, so whether enough reasoning would replace them with correct answers cannot be told at this cap. At high effort there are too few wrong answers to fit. Qwen3 was run at a single reasoning setting, and in the thinking condition it still makes the first-fit errors of claim 1. The predictive check: does the fitted model reproduce the data?

100 fake data sets are drawn from each fitted mixture (its weights, those below 0.01 set to 0, its ε and its ρ), on the same items with the same exclusions. Three simple statistics of the real answers are compared with the same statistics over the fake data sets: their mean and 5–95% range. An observed value outside that range means the fitted model does not generate answers like these. The fourth check is the distance profile: the shares of answers at each distance from the nearest member (0, 1, 2, 3, 4–6, more than 6, a special token), against the same shares in the fake data sets. Its statistic is the total variation distance from the fake data sets' mean profile, and p is the share of fake data sets at least as far from it as the real data. It fails at p < 0.05. A fit passes only if every check passes.

Why the fourth check. The first statistic can barely fail while ε is below its cap: the fit chooses ε so that the expected share of exact matches is close to the observed share. On C E2 knapsack the other two are 0 by construction (no special tokens, no answer out of reach), so the first three checks there amount to “ε is below its cap”. The distance profile tests the shape of the near misses, which no single parameter fits. Because the fake data come from parameters fitted to the real data, the real data sit closer to the fake data's mean than a fresh data set would: the check is conservative. Still, each fit faces four checks against 100 fake data sets, so a single marginal failure is weak evidence; the clear failures are those with ε at its cap or several checks failed. What this does and does not show

What the data show

  • Which fits pass the predictive checks against their data, and which do not (4 of 24 do, with ε below its cap). A pass is weak support, and a single marginal failure is weak evidence.
  • How often each model's wrong answers equal a member's wrong answer, on the gated units, against a within-cell permutation chance level where one exists.
  • Accuracy, validity and member-match counts on every unit and run.

What they do not show

  • Which procedure any model runs. These are agreements between answers. Identification also needs manipulation signatures, traces or process measures (DESIGN §3 O3), and none were analysed here.
  • Rates of heuristic use wherever a fit is labelled nearness, not use (20 of 24 fits).
  • Any difference between model families. Model and condition change together, and no cross-model fit or paired test has been run (DESIGN §5 rule 7).
  • That the zoo is complete. A small π₀ does not show it: in recovery, a withheld procedure's weight went mostly onto zoo members (RECOVERY.md, Off-zoo).
  • Anything about the units that are not fitted beyond their counts, or about the test split or other item sizes.

Caveats

  • Dev split, not the pre-registered test split. Nothing here was pre-registered: not the units, the statistics or the thresholds.
  • One sample per item. Run-to-run variance is not measured (DESIGN §5 rule 8). 8 pilot runs reused the paired dev run's own responses from the cache on the items the sets share, so those runs are not independent samples there.
  • The gates were checked at one operating point (a planted 0.6 / 0.3 / 0.1 triple, ε = 0.1, ρ = 0.3), which the real fits do not share. The recovery check at each fit's own estimate is in its panel.
  • The slip rate sits at its cap in 14 of 24 fits. The near-miss model wants more slip mass than the engine allows (EPS_MAX), so the answers miss the members more often, or by more, than the model can produce.
  • A weight is not a count of exact matches. πh includes h's near misses. Where no answer equals any member's, every weight on a member is near misses.
  • Percentile intervals are primary; BCa intervals from the same draws are in the tables. Neither is reliable on the boundary of the simplex (RECOVERY.md, Limitations): with π₀ = 1 they under-cover on C E2 knapsack, and coverage for a weight that sits at 0 was not checked.
  • Selection. The items are built so that members disagree. The weights describe these items, not instances at large (RECOVERY.md, Limitations).
  • Truncated responses are censored and excluded; the fits have no token model to correct for that.
  • The direct condition's instruction conflicts with the prompts: the system message says to answer without working, the prompts say to think as carefully as needed (RUNNER.md, Open questions).
  • Served at fp8: Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B. Quantised serving may change behaviour against the released weights (RUNNER.md, provider pins).
